Back at the Wheel

New pottery, special commission work, and holiday news from the Garden Shed

Hello, friends!

It's been an eventful several months here at Garden Shed Clay, and I'm delighted to have some news to share from the studio.

Back at the Wheel

After spending time recovering from a rotator cuff and shoulder injury and working through physical therapy, I'm finally back at the pottery wheel and making pots again.

There's something particularly satisfying about returning to clay after being away from it. The familiar rhythm of wedging, throwing, trimming, glazing, and firing feels comfortable and reassuring.

Recovery has required so much patience. I'm happy and relieved to be creating in my studio again and hard at work on exciting new projects.

Pottery with a Special Purpose

One of the most meaningful projects I've been working on involves Lovefeast mugs for Mountain Laurel Moravian Fellowship, created in memory of David Piner.

David was a cornerstone member of Home Moravian Church in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, and our families go back generations. David's mother, Jean, and I were very close, and his unexpected passing was a terrible shock.

I felt a calling to prepare this gift of love for the Mountain Laurel fellowship, where David and Ted had been pouring so much love.

It's been an honor to make pottery that carries such personal significance and will become part of a community's shared traditions. How fortunate I am.

I'm also delighted to share that Garden Shed Clay was awarded another pottery commission from the Institute for Moravian History and World Heritage at Moravian University. That work will be delivered in the coming week.

It's exciting to continue creating pottery connected to these traditions and communities.

Interested in learning more? Visit Mountain Laurel Moravian Fellowship or the Institute for Moravian History and World Heritage.

Fresh from the Studio

With production underway again, several new pieces are taking shape.

Among the latest work are cups, chip and dip bowls, pie plates, bird and bee water dishes, bread-warming tiles, and other designs, including pieces featuring a speckled rainbow glaze.

I've been enjoying the opportunity to experiment with forms and finishes while building up the collection for the coming season.

As always, every piece is made by hand, so no two are quite alike.

A New Home for Garden Shed Clay

Garden Shed Clay now has its own independent website!

You can explore the current pottery collection, read stories from the studio and garden, and learn more about the work.

Explore Garden Shed Clay

It's wonderful to have a home for Garden Shed Clay that brings everything together in one place.

Looking Ahead to the Holidays

The holiday season is approaching, and the studio is busy!

If you're thinking about giving handmade pottery as a gift—or ordering something special for your own table—now is a good time to take a look at the collection.

Because each piece is handmade and shipping takes time, I'll be establishing a holiday ordering cutoff to give orders the best possible chance of arriving in time.

I'll share that date as soon as it's confirmed, but right now it looks like it will be the second week of December.

Thank you, as always, for following along, supporting handmade pottery, and being part of the Garden Shed Clay community.

It's good to be playing in the mud once again!

Love,
Lester
