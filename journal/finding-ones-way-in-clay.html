Finding One’s Way in Clay

On discovering ceramics, learning the material, and making pottery meant to become part of everyday life.

August 30, 2026 · Lester Morris

I discovered ceramics in 1999, when I was looking for a creative outlet outside of my workdays in Charlotte, North Carolina. The local community college offered a class, and I enrolled. From that moment, I was moved by the material and knew it would be a part of my life in some form.

Through clay, I connected with other potters and creative people, built relationships, and was influenced by their ideas and designs. Over time, those influences became part of my own designs and development as an artist. You borrow from others, you put in your own ability and ideas, and the output becomes something uniquely yours.

That evolves over time, but I think there is a constant thread of the creative self that permeates and is present in each piece. A friend of mine used to call it “finding one’s way in clay,” and I like that a lot. I’m not sure what I will be making in the future, but I really enjoy where I am with it and what I am doing now.

What I Love About Making Pottery

Clay is moldable and incredibly versatile. I love working in three dimensions, thinking of ideas for pottery, and then using my hands to bring them into reality.

The act of throwing on a wheel is also totally relaxing and meditative for me. I often tell friends that if I am having a bad day or am conflicted in my thoughts, I cannot center the clay on the wheel. I have to focus on centering myself to make it happen.

That meditative, centering approach translates into being more methodical and thoughtful, and moving at a slower and more deliberate pace. That brings out the best work in me. Maybe others are not this way, but it is how I work best and what I like to do.

I also love the continuity of pottery. I can look at the work of other artisans and see the material from the unique perspective of working with it myself. I know the material.

One of the highlights of my life was seeing Jōmon pottery in a gallery during a trip to Japan. I could see fingerprints and marks in the clay made by people thousands of years ago, and I immediately felt a connection with them from the standpoint of knowing the material myself. Their hands worked with the same material that mine do. To be able to recognize that across such an enormous span of time is a real gift.

Why I Make What I Make

I enjoy making practical and functional pieces that are meant to be used. Familiar objects like mugs and plates are things we connect with on a daily basis. Sometimes a mug may have a special meaning or mark a point in time or remind us of a person, and that adds something to the experience of using it.

I also enjoy fine art pottery, but lately I have been focused on practical pieces for the kitchen and garden. I like the idea of making something by hand that becomes part of someone else’s everyday life.

What I Hope You Take From My Work

When you open a box containing a pot from Garden Shed Clay, I hope it is something that is exciting for you. I hope you know that it was handmade with love and care and that it was made especially for you.

I hope you use it.

And I hope that, in some small way, it brings something special and nice into your daily life.
